Indian Bus Conductor Offers Solution to Airline Seating Disputes

A government bus conductor proposes an unconventional solution to prolonged arguments aboard international flights.

By The Banana Times Staff · October 22, 2026 · 1 min read

NEW DELHI — An Indian government bus conductor has questioned the aviation industry's approach to passenger disputes after reports that a woman cried for three hours aboard an international flight when another passenger declined to exchange seats with her husband.

“This is why we don't assign seats,” the conductor said. “If a couple wants to sit together, they can ask someone to move. If nobody agrees, they can stand together.”

The conductor acknowledged that disputes occasionally arise on buses but said they are generally resolved without prolonged disruption.

“If an argument gets out of hand, we simply stop the bus, drop both parties at a random location and continue our journey.”

He suggested that airlines consider adopting a similar approach, including providing parachutes to passengers involved in particularly persistent disagreements.

“They can settle their differences in midair,” he said. “There is no reason to inconvenience everyone else.”
