AI Durga Pandal Faces First Crisis Over Conflicting Prayers

A virtual Durga Puja experiment promised fewer queues, less waste and faster access to blessings. Its first serious problem came from members of the same family.

By The Banana Times Staff · Kolkata · October 6, 2026

KOLKATA — A Durga Puja committee led by young organizers has introduced a virtual pandal designed to reduce traffic, waste and long queues by allowing visitors to enter through virtual-reality headsets.

The project also includes an artificial-intelligence system that allows devotees to offer prayers digitally.

During testing, however, organizers encountered a problem that had less to do with technology than with family life.

A mother asked Maa Durga to help her son secure a permanent government job in Kolkata.

Minutes later, the son’s wife entered the virtual pandal from another headset and prayed that her husband be transferred as far from Kolkata as possible.

“Conflicting blessings detected. Indian values prevent us from choosing sides.”

The developers said they had anticipated server failures, connectivity problems and periods of unusually high demand. They had not anticipated contradictory prayers from members of the same household.

“We trained the system on religion, philosophy and government recruitment rules,” one developer said. “We did not train it for Indian families.”

Traditional pandal makers have viewed the experiment with some skepticism. The virtual project requires no bamboo, elaborate temporary structures or physical decorations, something its organizers have promoted as an environmental advantage.

One craftsman was less impressed.

“At a real pandal,” he said, “both prayers would simply remain pending. There would be no text notification about the conflict — because that only creates problems within families.”

A religious influencer later offered a broader interpretation of the episode.

“We are constantly praying for things that cancel each other out,” he said. “One person wants rain, another wants sunshine. One wants a promotion, another wants the same promotion.”

“We are still a developing nation because even our prayers have not reached a common consensus,” he said. “We must progress together as a society.”
