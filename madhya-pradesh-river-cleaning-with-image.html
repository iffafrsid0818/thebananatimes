Residents Seek Government Recognition for ‘Efficient’ River-Cleaning System

Residents say a volunteer’s eight-month cleanup has created an efficient arrangement: they provide the garbage, and he provides the cleaning.

By The Banana Times Staff · October 14, 2026

MADHYA PRADESH — Residents of a small town are asking the government to recognize what they describe as a model of civic cooperation, after an environmental volunteer spent eight months removing rubbish from their local river.

The volunteer cleared tonnes of plastic, discarded clothing and other waste, leaving long stretches of the river unusually clean.

Residents say the arrangement has since become highly efficient.

“He removes it, we put it back,” one resident said. “Everyone has a role.”

Another said the system had made waste disposal easier.

“It is much easier than finding a kachra dumping ground,” he said. “The river is nearby, and now somebody is cleaning it.”

A proud resident said the town deserved a government award.

“There is cooperation and division of labour,” he said. “We provide the garbage. He provides the cleaning.”

He also defended the arrangement on economic grounds.

“If we stop throwing garbage, what will he clean?” he asked. “We have to think about livelihoods. It is also good for his YouTube channel.”

The volunteer has since resumed cleaning the river.

Residents said they hoped the system would continue.

“The problem,” one man said, “has become very well organized.”
