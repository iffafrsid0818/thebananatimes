Police Physical Test Updated After Viral Van-Key Incident

New training standards will require recruits to chase an instructor who grabs a police van key from a variety of positions, with a viral incident providing the minimum running-speed benchmark.

By The Banana Times Staff · October 5, 2026 · 1 min read

NEW DELHI — Police training standards are being revised to introduce a minimum running speed for officers responding to unexpected situations, following the viral video of a woman taking the key to a police van and running away.

Under the new physical test, recruits will first be required to sit comfortably inside a stationary police van. Without warning, an instructor will grab the vehicle’s key and begin running.

The exercise will be repeated with the key grabbed from different positions — including while the officer is sitting in the driver’s seat, standing beside the van, talking to another officer and looking in the opposite direction.

The recruit must immediately leave the van and pursue the instructor.

Officials said footage of the original incident had been analyzed to calculate the woman’s running speed, which will now serve as the minimum qualifying standard.

“We finally have a realistic benchmark,” an official familiar with the proposed test said.

Recruits who fail to catch the instructor will be given a second attempt.

They will not, however, be given a second key.
