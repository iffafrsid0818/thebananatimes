Detainees Protest After Delhi Police Introduces Strict Samosa Eligibility Rules

A disagreement over who qualified for refreshments after detention has grown into a campaign for clearer standards, equal treatment and a precise definition of the human back.

By The Banana Times Staff · New Delhi · October 13, 2026

NEW DELHI — A group of former detainees gathered to protest what they described as unequal access to samosas following a recent police action in the capital.

The dispute began after one detainee said he noticed that several people around him had been given samosas while he had received nothing.

“When I asked why, they told me samosas were only for people who had received a lathi on the back,” he said.

The man said he had also been struck and initially assumed that he qualified. But, according to his account, officials determined that only half of the resulting mark was on his back. The other half was on his buttocks.

“I was told this made me ineligible,” he said. “Apparently the benefit is location-specific.”

Other detainees described similarly complicated cases. One said a mark extending from his shoulder to his upper arm had been classified as “mixed use.” Another said two lighter blows could not be combined to meet the threshold for one qualifying blow.

The protesters said they were not seeking preferential treatment. They wanted, they said, a transparent system.

“If there is an eligibility rule, publish it,” one protester said. “People should know before getting hit whether they are working toward a samosa.”

By afternoon, another pattern had become a subject of discussion among the group. Some detainees who had received samosas said they had encountered no difficulty confirming their voting eligibility. Several who had gone without said their records remained “under review.”

Authorities said any connection between the two matters was purely coincidental.

“First they said the samosa had nothing to do with the lathi,” one protester said. “Now they are saying it has nothing to do with voting. At this point, I would just like to know what exactly the samosa is for.”

The demonstrators have since adopted a slogan: “One Lathi, One Samosa.”

They said the protest would continue until authorities either distributed the snacks equally or published an official diagram showing precisely where a person must be struck to qualify.
