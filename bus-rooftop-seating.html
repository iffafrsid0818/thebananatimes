Bus Manufacturer Introduces Rooftop Seating After Railways Reinforce Train Roofs for Passengers

A new proposal promises safety belts, additional capacity and a fresh definition of public-transport progress.

By The Banana Times Staff · October 16, 2026

NEW DELHI — Following reports that Indian railway engineers have designed reinforced train roofs to accommodate passengers travelling on top, a leading bus manufacturer has announced plans to introduce rooftop seating with safety belts, describing the innovation as a major breakthrough in public transportation.

“For decades, people have travelled on bus roofs without proper seating arrangements,” a company spokesperson said. “We believe they deserve the same comfort as passengers inside.”

The announcement was welcomed by social media influencer Jogi Baba, who praised India's remarkable technological progress.

“After independence, overcrowding was a major problem in public transport,” Baba told The Banana Times. “Nearly 80 years later, we have finally found a solution. Instead of preventing people from travelling on roofs, we are making it safer. This is progress.”

Asked whether introducing more buses and wider roads might offer a more conventional solution, Baba dismissed the suggestion as unnecessarily expensive.

“We cannot keep solving problems by addressing their underlying causes,” he said. “That is not innovation.”

Baba added that he looked forward to taking a ride as soon as the new buses became available, promising to document the journey for his followers.

“I want to show the world the strength of our technology and how far we have progressed as a society,” he said.

The manufacturer confirmed that rooftop passengers would pay the same fare as those inside, although premium seating with unobstructed views of the sky would cost extra.
