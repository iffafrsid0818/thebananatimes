IISc Professors Hired to Investigate Possible Space-Time Problem in Bengaluru Traffic

Conventional explanations exhausted; Einstein called in to explain the morning commute.

By The Banana Times Staff · Bengaluru

BENGALURU: After years of unsuccessful attempts to solve Bengaluru's traffic problems using flyovers, traffic signals, one-way roads and increasingly desperate Google Maps routes, authorities have reportedly turned to theoretical physics.

A team of professors from the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) has been asked to investigate whether Bengaluru's traffic congestion may actually be caused by a previously unknown disturbance in space-time.

A theoretical physicist involved in the study said conventional explanations could no longer account for observations made on Bengaluru roads.

“We have examined vehicle density, road capacity, signal timing and human behavior. At this point, space-time distortion is the only reasonable explanation left.”

The research began after scientists noticed several cases in which commuters left home at 8:30 a.m., travelled approximately seven kilometres, and arrived at work shortly before lunch.

According to the professor, his mathematical theory is now “almost complete” and uses Einstein's theory of relativity to explain why a 20-minute journey shown on Google Maps can take two hours without technically violating the laws of physics.

“If our calculations are correct, Einstein's theory of relativity will, for the first time, be tested under actual Bengaluru traffic conditions.”

Researchers are particularly interested in Silk Board Junction, where preliminary calculations suggest that time may pass normally for pedestrians standing outside the junction while slowing considerably for anyone sitting inside a car.

One experiment reportedly involved two identical clocks. One was kept inside the IISc laboratory while another was placed inside a cab travelling toward Electronic City.

The laboratory clock reached 4 p.m.

The cab reached the next signal.

Scientists have cautioned that further research is necessary before concluding that Bengaluru contains a genuine space-time anomaly. However, the professor said the team had already obtained one result that conventional physics was unable to explain.

“We sent one researcher to Whitefield last Tuesday,” he said.

“He hasn't reached yet.”
